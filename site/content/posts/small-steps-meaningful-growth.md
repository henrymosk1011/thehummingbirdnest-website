Every child grows in their own way, at their own pace.

As parents, it can be easy to compare. We hear about what another child is doing, see milestones listed by age, or wonder why our child seems to be taking longer with something that appears to come easily to others.

But development is not a straight line.

Children do not simply move from one milestone to the next in a predictable order. Development is more like a winding trail, with progress, pauses, unexpected leaps, and sometimes even moments that seem like steps backward.

And that's okay.

## Progress Can Look Different From What We Expect

One of the most important things we can remember is that progress does not always look the way we imagined it would.

A child may learn a new skill and then need extra support using it in a different setting. They may communicate more clearly at home than at school. They may become more independent with one task while still needing significant help with another.

These differences do not mean a child is not growing.

Development occurs in many areas at the same time including communication, social emotional skills, motor skills, problem solving, self regulation, independence, and relationships. A child may be making meaningful progress in one area while another area is still developing.

Sometimes, the biggest changes are the ones that are easy to overlook.

Maybe your child stayed with an activity a little longer. Maybe they asked for help instead of becoming frustrated. Maybe they tolerated a change in routine that would have been overwhelming a few months ago. Maybe they made an attempt to connect with another child.

These moments matter.

## Comparing Can Make Progress Harder to See

It is natural to compare.

As parents, we want to know that our children are growing and developing, and milestones can be helpful information.

But sometimes comparisons can move us from "What is my child learning?" to "Why isn't my child doing what other children are doing?"

Instead, try looking at your child's own starting point.

Ask:

* What can my child do now that they couldn't do before?
* What are they showing interest in?
* What situations help them feel successful?
* What skills are beginning to emerge?
* What support helps them participate more independently?

Viewing growth from this perspective makes progress more apparent.

## Reaching Children Where They Are

Meeting a child where they are is not watering down expectations or abandoning growth. That is, understanding where they are coming from and building on that.

By meeting children where they are developmentally, we can create opportunities that are developmentally appropriate, but challenging enough to be inviting, and not overwhelming enough to shut down.

It might mean changing how we communicate, giving a child more time to respond, breaking a task down into smaller steps, following their interests, or supporting them before we expect them to be independent.

The goal is not to rush children towards where we think they should be.

The goal is to help them build the skills they need to move forward.

## There Is No One "Right" Timeline

Every child brings their own strengths, needs, personality, experiences, and ways of learning. Two children the same age can reach the same skill in completely different ways and at completely different times.

A developmental milestone can be a helpful guide, but it should never become a measure of a child's worth.

Your child's development is not a race. There is no prize for getting there first.

What matters is that your child has the support, connection, and opportunities they need to continue growing.

## Looking for the Small Steps

Sometimes growth is so gradual we don't even notice until we look back.

A child who once refused to do an activity may now participate for a few minutes. A child who previously had difficulty communicating their needs may begin to use a gesture, word, picture, or other form of communication. A child who once needed constant adult support may begin taking the first step independently.

These may seem like small moments, but development is built through small moments.

Growth does not have to be dramatic to be meaningful.

At The Hummingbird Nest, we believe in meeting children where they are and supporting them from there. We celebrate the small steps, the emerging skills, the moments of connection, and the progress that does not always fit neatly into a checklist.

Development is not about hitting every milestone all at once. It is about helping each child to grow to their next step.
